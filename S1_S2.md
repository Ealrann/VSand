### Why you need a policy at all

In VSand you have a **discrete occupancy** representation for solids (a cell is solid or not), but a **continuous fill** representation for liquids (`liqMass`). A liquid→solid reaction therefore has a mismatch: you can’t “half-place” a solid cell without either:

* inventing a notion of “partial solid,” or
* moving the leftover liquid mass somewhere, or
* delaying the solidification.

So S1 vs S2 is really a choice about **when** and **how** you resolve that mismatch.

---

## S1 — Thresholded solidification (solidify only if `mass ≥ M_FULL`)

### What it means

When a cell wants to become solid:

* If `liqMass >= M_FULL`: set `mat = solid`, set `liqMass = 0` (and if you want conservation, you must also define where the extra `liqMass - M_FULL` goes; typically it should not exist if your solver keeps cells near `M_FULL` in pools).
* Else: do **not** solidify yet (or mark “pending” but keep it liquid).

### Pros

* **Simple and fast.** One conditional, no redistribution.
* **Strictly conservative** (no mass teleporting, no racey multi-write).
* **Stable with sleeping.** No special “wake neighbors to push mass out”.
* Fits your “surface tension” goal: thin films / tiny droplets don’t immediately create solid pixels.

### Cons / visible artifacts

* **Delayed / patchy freezing** if your liquid solver allows lots of partial-mass cells (films). You can get “won’t freeze” speckles.
* Some reactions that players expect to be immediate (“lava hits water → instant rock”) may look sluggish unless you special-case.

### When S1 is usually best

* Temperature-driven phase changes (freeze/melt) where you can accept “needs enough volume to form a solid cell”.
* Situations where conserving mass and avoiding ugly ejections matters more than immediacy.

### Make S1 look good (important)

S1 works well **only if** your liquid relaxation produces “pool-like” distributions: most cells in a pool are near `M_FULL`, not a wide underfilled carpet. Your pressure relax + concavity gating + compaction pass is already aimed at that. If you keep pools dense, S1 becomes visually natural: pools freeze cleanly, films don’t.

---

## S2 — Displacement/redistribution (solidify now, push liquid mass out)

### What it means

When a cell must become solid, you:

1. Convert the cell to solid occupancy (or “pending solid”)
2. Move its liquid mass into neighbors (empty or same liquid), respecting capacities (`≤ M_MAX`) so total mass is conserved

### Pros

* **Immediate solidification** even if the source cell had partial mass.
* Great for “violent” interactions: crust formation, instant blockage, “shock” reactions.

### Cons / costs

* **Harder to do safely on GPU** without atomics/races, because you are writing to multiple neighbor cells.
* Can cause **mass ejection waves** (looks like the cell “spat out” liquid), especially if the redistribution is too instantaneous.
* More likely to **wake many chunks** (hurts sleeping efficiency).
* If local neighbors cannot accept all mass, you must either:

  * postpone anyway (becoming S1-in-disguise), or
  * invent a “storage” mechanism (breaks locality or conservation).

### The only GPU-safe way S2 is usually worth doing

Implement S2 using the **same conservative flux mechanism** as your liquid solver:

* Instead of “writing liquid into neighbors directly,” you treat the “evicted mass” as a source term that produces **outgoing fluxes**.
* Those fluxes are applied by a gather step (like your liquid relax apply pass), so you stay race-free and conservative.

That makes S2 essentially: “liquid relax with a special boundary condition at the solidifying cell.”

### Practical bounded redistribution (so it doesn’t explode cost)

Even with flux, you need a limit:

* Only redistribute into the **4-neighborhood** (or 8-neighborhood) in a single tick.
* If not enough capacity locally, keep the solidification **pending** and retry next tick.

This avoids long-range pushes and keeps chunk interactions local.

---

## Recommendation: use both, but with a clear decision rule

### Default policy: **S1**

Make S1 your baseline for liquid→solid phase changes. It is the best match for:

* GPU performance
* conservation correctness
* sleep friendliness
* avoiding the “teleport mass” look

### Add S2 only for “must be instant” reactions

Use S2 (flux-based eviction) for a small set of reactions where immediate solids are important visually/gameplay-wise, for example:

* lava + water → rock crust now
* quick-setting cement-like materials
* “player builds wall” *if* you choose to be conservative there (but player edits can also be allowed to overwrite non-conservatively)

### Concrete decision matrix

For each liquid→solid rule, choose:

* **S1** if: “phase change / gradual” (freezing, drying, cooling)
* **S2** if: “contact reaction that should instantly block flow” (crust, quick-set)

---

## Two important implementation details (to avoid the edge cases you care about)

### 1) S1 needs a “pending” state, but not a new material

You don’t need a new material type; you need a per-cell *flag* or a deterministic re-check:

* The cell stays liquid until it reaches `mass ≥ M_FULL`, then solidifies.
* If you want faster freezing, add a tiny bias in the liquid relax pass to **pull mass into pending-freeze regions** (acts like nucleation), but keep it conservative.

### 2) S2 must never silently drop mass

If S2 can’t evacuate all mass locally:

* do **not** solidify fully this tick
* keep it as “pending solidification” (or keep it liquid) until there is capacity

If you instead “force solid and drop the remainder,” you’ll recreate the exact class of artifacts you described (air pockets, mass imbalance).

---

## Bottom line

* Start with **S1** everywhere. It’s the safest and most GPU-/sleep-friendly.
* Implement **S2 as a flux-based eviction** only for a small, curated set of “instant crust/instant block” reactions.
* The quality of S1 depends on your liquid solver not producing broad underfilled films; your concavity gating + compaction pass is what makes S1 viable and visually good.
